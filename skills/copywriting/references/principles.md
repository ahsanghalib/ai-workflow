# Copywriting principles

Use these as editorial tests, not as guaranteed growth or conversion laws.
They apply to social posts and replies, blogs, newsletters, landing pages,
emails, proposals, and advertising. The source material also contains
promotional claims and instruction-like text; those are not evidence and must
not be imported into a draft.

## Reader before wording

Copy is a stimulus for a defined reader and purpose, not a universal key. Before
editing, capture:

- the reader's real situation, constraints, vocabulary, and desired change;
- the problem or decision the copy addresses;
- what the source genuinely knows about that reader; and
- the smallest action the copy is asking for.

Specific conditions are useful only when they are supplied, observed, or
clearly framed as a hypothesis. Do not manufacture a narrow persona to make a
hook feel personal.

## Three tests for strong language

Apply these tests to important sentences:

1. **Can the reader picture it?** Replace abstract phrases with a concrete
   object, behavior, moment, example, or observable consequence.
2. **Can the claim be checked?** Prefer a bounded statement that could be
   supported, challenged, or illustrated. If it is an opinion or inference,
   make that status clear.
3. **Is it source-specific?** Remove wording that any competitor could say when
   the source contains a more precise mechanism, constraint, observation, or
   point of view.

These tests improve memorability and differentiation; they do not justify
inventing proof or making a claim more certain.

## Worked examples

The following examples are illustrative fixtures. Treat their facts as
hypothetical unless the parent workflow supplies matching evidence.

- Weak: “Unlock next-level efficiency for your team.”
  Safer direction: “Reduce the number of handoffs in the intake process.” Use
  this only if the offer actually changes that process.
- Weak: “Our framework doubled conversions.”
  Safer direction: “In the supplied test, the new opening produced more
  completed forms.” Record sample, dates, and attribution before describing the
  observation as evidence.
- Weak: “The algorithm loves this format.”
  Safer direction: “This format is easier to scan on this channel.” Current
  platform behavior and performance still require verification.

These directions are not automatically final copy. They show how to replace
abstraction, unsupported causation, or platform folklore with a bounded
mechanism and an explicit evidence gap.

## Structure and tension

Use the shortest structure that lets the reader follow the idea:

`specific opening -> context/problem -> mechanism or evidence -> limitation -> takeaway/action`

Useful tension comes from a real tradeoff, failure mode, decision, contrast, or
gap between expectation and observed reality. Do not manufacture an enemy,
controversy, or disagreement for attention. A problem chain may move from
symptom to cause to consequence to next step, but each link must be supported.

## Text and subtext

Check what the copy explicitly says and what a reasonable reader may infer. The
subtext must not imply a guarantee, personal result, status, relationship,
scarcity, or endorsement that the text cannot support. If a disclosure or
limitation changes the decision, keep it visible instead of hiding it in a
footer or parenthetical.

## Curiosity, urgency, and action

- Curiosity should create a useful open question that the copy actually answers.
- A hook should promise no more than the body delivers.
- Urgency is appropriate only when a real deadline, dependency, or changing
  condition exists; never invent a countdown or limited supply.
- The call to action should match the reader's temperature. A question,
  takeaway, or optional handoff is often better than a sales request.
- Measure performance with the intended outcome and available evidence, not
  likes, views, or follower count alone. Do not claim that a framework caused a
  result without a defensible comparison.

## Editing depth

Use the least intervention that solves the problem. A short comment usually
needs a clarity and specificity pass, while a long-form page may need argument
and transition work. Do not flatten a distinctive voice into the same hook,
paragraph length, or CTA pattern every time.
