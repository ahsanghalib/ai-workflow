# Evidence and testing

Use this reference when explaining why an edit was made, diagnosing observed
performance, or proposing a test. It keeps durable craft principles separate
from claims about conversion across social posts, blogs, newsletters, and
advertising.

## Durable evidence

### Test the message against a real response

Claude C. Hopkins's *Scientific Advertising* (1923) is a historical primary
source for treating advertising as an empirical discipline: know the product,
state the reason to believe, measure response, and compare alternatives rather
than trusting taste. Use that principle as a testing posture, not as proof that
an old advertising tactic will work on a current channel.

Source: [Library of Congress record and public-domain scan](https://www.loc.gov/item/23009362/).

### Clarity and scanability have measured usability value

Nielsen Norman Group's web-writing studies are relevant to comprehension, not
automatic sales. One 1997 study reported that 79% of participants scanned new
pages and 16% read word by word. In the same report, concise, scannable, and
objective rewrites improved measured usability by 58%, 47%, and 27% separately,
and 124% in combination. A later white-paper rewrite study with 21 technical
users reported a 159% overall usability improvement. These are old, bounded
usability studies—not universal conversion benchmarks.

Sources: [How Users Read on the Web](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/)
and [Applying Writing Guidelines to Web Pages](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/applying-writing-guidelines-web-pages/).

Operational rule: make copy concise, scannable, and objective when the reader
must find, understand, or act on information. Preserve necessary detail and
test the tradeoff when the artifact needs emotion, narrative, or nuance.

### Persuasion must remain truthful

Influence At Work publishes Robert Cialdini's code of ethics: be truthful,
avoid manipulating people or facts, use principles that genuinely exist in the
situation, use relevant contrast, and educate people into agreement. The
associated paper distinguishes legitimate expertise, accurate social
validation, real similarity, useful favors, and legitimate scarcity from traps
or deception.

Sources: [Cialdini Code of Ethics](https://www.influenceatwork.com/about-iaw/)
and [Cialdini-in-6.0 paper](https://www.influenceatwork.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/02/Cornell-HotelRestAdminQrtly.pdf).

## Testing protocol

When real traffic or user research is available:

1. State one falsifiable hypothesis: “Changing X for reader Y should improve
   action Z because mechanism M is clearer.”
2. Change one primary message variable where practical: opening, promise,
   mechanism, proof, objection handling, offer framing, or CTA.
3. Predefine the primary outcome and a guardrail. Use qualified replies,
   completed tasks, demo requests, or revenue only when attribution is
   observable; do not substitute likes or impressions without saying so.
4. Keep audience, placement, timing, and offer as comparable as practical.
5. Record sample, dates, exposure, exclusions, and anomalies. Do not claim a
   winner from a tiny or confounded observation.
6. Report the result as observed, inconclusive, or conflicting. Treat a win as
   local evidence for the tested context, not a universal rule.

Without real response data, perform a source-grounded editorial review and
label all performance expectations as hypotheses.

## Example hypotheses by channel

Use these as shapes for a test plan, not as expected results:

- **Social opening:** Naming the concrete handoff delay instead of saying
  “improve efficiency” should increase qualified replies because the problem
  is easier for the intended reader to recognize. Primary outcome: qualified
  replies. Guardrail: unqualified or misleading clicks.
- **Blog headline:** Leading with the reader's decision rather than the tool
  name should increase useful article engagement because the searcher's problem
  is clearer. Primary outcome: completion of the intended article action.
  Guardrail: no increase in superficial clicks with lower completion.
- **Advertisement:** Replacing a broad benefit with the supplied deliverable
  should increase qualified form submissions because the offer is easier to
  evaluate. Primary outcome: qualified submissions. Guardrail: rejection rate,
  refund requests, or other supplied quality measure.

For each example, keep the audience, offer, placement, timing, and destination
stable where practical. Do not infer causation from a platform's impressions,
likes, or a single unqualified click spike.

## Claims that require special restraint

Do not repeat popular percentages about awareness levels, conversion lifts,
algorithm preferences, “viral” formulas, or revenue multipliers unless the
parent workflow has a named primary source, method, date, and matching context.
