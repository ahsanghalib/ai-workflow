# Humanizer pattern reference

Read this reference when the text needs a thorough audit. Look for clusters,
not isolated words or punctuation choices.

## Content and reasoning

- Inflated significance, legacy, symbolism, or broad-trend claims.
- Media-coverage and notability lists without useful context.
- Superficial `-ing` analyses such as "highlighting", "showcasing", or
  "contributing to" that add no mechanism or evidence.
- Promotional language, vague authorities, and unsupported expert claims.
- Formulaic "challenges and future prospects" sections.
- Speculative gap-filling, especially private-person biography or claims based
  only on a supposed knowledge gap.

## Language and structure

- Repeated AI vocabulary: *delve*, *crucial*, *pivotal*, *landscape*,
  *showcase*, *tapestry*, *testament*, *underscore*, *vibrant*, and similar
  words when plainer language would do.
- Elaborate substitutes for `is`, `are`, `has`, and `can`.
- Negative parallelisms, clipped tailing negations, forced groups of three,
  synonym cycling, and meaningless "from X to Y" ranges.
- Passive voice or subjectless fragments when the actor matters.
- Filler, excessive hedging, authority tropes, signposting, generic conclusions,
  and repeated hyphenated compounds.
- Diff narration in documents that should describe the current state.
- Stacked fragments, manufactured punchlines, aphorism formulas, and fake-
  candid openers such as "Honestly?" or "Here's the thing."

## Formatting and conversation

- Em/en dash overuse, mechanical boldface, inline-header lists, title-case
  headings, decorative emojis, or curly quotes used as part of a larger cluster.
- Chatbot artifacts such as "I hope this helps", "Great question", or offers to
  continue when the text is supposed to be the actual deliverable.
- Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers that replace an answer with speculation.

## Preserve these signals

Do not flatten specific unusual details, unresolved tension, dated references,
defensible first-person choices, varied rhythm, genuine asides, or deliberate
quirks. Correct formatting and formal vocabulary are not evidence by
themselves. Do not rewrite watched phrases inside quotations, titles, names, or
examples discussing the phrase.

## Representative before-and-after examples

Use these examples to recognize the pattern in context. Preserve the source's
facts and do not invent the more specific wording shown in an example.

## Watch phrases by pattern

- **Significance:** *stands as*, *serves as*, *testament*, *vital*,
  *significant*, *crucial*, *pivotal*, *underscores*, *broader movement*,
  *symbolizing*, *setting the stage*, *marking a shift*, *evolving landscape*,
  *focal point*, *indelible mark*, *deeply rooted*.
- **Notability:** *independent coverage*, *local/regional/national media*,
  *leading expert*, *active social media presence*.
- **Superficial -ing:** *highlighting*, *underscoring*, *emphasizing*,
  *ensuring*, *reflecting*, *symbolizing*, *contributing*, *cultivating*,
  *fostering*, *encompassing*, *showcasing*.
- **Promotion:** *boasts*, *vibrant*, figurative *rich* or *profound*,
  *enhancing*, *showcasing*, *exemplifies*, *commitment to*, *natural beauty*,
  *nestled*, *in the heart of*, *groundbreaking*, *renowned*, *breathtaking*,
  *must-visit*, *stunning*.
- **Vague authority:** *industry reports*, *observers have cited*, *experts
  argue*, *some critics argue*, and *several sources/publications* when few
  are named.
- **Formulaic challenges:** *despite its*, *faces several challenges*,
  *despite these challenges*, *challenges and legacy*, *future outlook*.
- **High-frequency AI vocabulary:** *actually*, *additionally*, *align with*,
  *crucial*, *delve*, *emphasizing*, *enduring*, *enhance*, *fostering*,
  *garner*, *highlight*, *interplay*, *intricate*, *key*, *landscape*,
  *pivotal*, *showcase*, *tapestry*, *testament*, *underscore*, *valuable*,
  *vibrant*.
- **Other clusters:** copula avoidance (*serves as*, *stands as*, *features*,
  *boasts*); negative parallelism (*not only...but*, *not just...*); false
  ranges (*from X to Y* without a meaningful scale); cutoff or gap-fill
  phrases (*as of*, *based on available information*, *maintains a low
  profile*, *likely*); filler and signposting (*in order to*, *due to the fact
  that*, *let's dive in*, *here's what you need to know*); and fake-candid
  openers (*Honestly?*, *Here's the thing*, *Real talk*).

### Significance, notability, and superficial analysis

- **Undue significance:** “The Statistical Institute of Catalonia was
  officially established in 1989, marking a pivotal moment in the evolution of
  regional statistics in Spain. This initiative was part of a broader movement
  across Spain to decentralize administrative functions and enhance regional
  governance.” Prefer: “The Statistical Institute of Catalonia was established
  in 1989, part of a wider decentralization of administrative functions in
  Spain.”
- **Media lists:** “Her views have been cited in The New York Times, BBC,
  Financial Times, and The Hindu. She maintains an active social media presence
  with over 500,000 followers.” Prefer a useful subset such as: “Her views
  have been cited in The New York Times and the BBC.” Keep one citation with
  real context when the source provides it; do not invent context.
- **Trailing -ing analysis:** “The temple's color palette of blue, green, and
  gold resonates with the region's natural beauty, symbolizing Texas
  bluebonnets, the Gulf of Mexico, and the diverse Texan landscapes, reflecting
  the community's deep connection to the land.” Prefer: “The temple is painted
  blue, green, and gold, colors meant to evoke Texas bluebonnets and the Gulf
  of Mexico.”
- **Promotion:** “Nestled within the breathtaking region of Gonder in
  Ethiopia, Alamata Raya Kobo stands as a vibrant town with a rich cultural
  heritage and stunning natural beauty.” Prefer: “Alamata Raya Kobo is a town
  in the Gonder region of Ethiopia.”
- **Vague attribution:** “Due to its unique characteristics, the Haolai River
  is of interest to researchers and conservationists. Experts believe it plays
  a crucial role in the regional ecosystem.” Prefer: “Researchers and
  conservationists study the Haolai River for its unusual characteristics.”
  Name a real source when one exists. Cut unsupported claims rather than
  decorating them.
- **Formulaic challenges section:** “Despite its industrial prosperity,
  Korattur faces challenges typical of urban areas, including traffic
  congestion and water scarcity. Despite these challenges, with its strategic
  location and ongoing initiatives, Korattur continues to thrive as an
  integral part of Chennai's growth.” Prefer: “Korattur has recurring traffic
  congestion and water shortages.” Use specifics supplied by the source or
  user, not by the rewrite.

### Language and grammar

- **AI vocabulary:** “Additionally, a distinctive feature of Somali cuisine is
  the incorporation of camel meat. An enduring testament to Italian colonial
  influence is the widespread adoption of pasta in the local culinary
  landscape, showcasing how these dishes have integrated into the traditional
  diet.” Prefer: “Somali cuisine also includes camel meat, which is considered
  a delicacy. Pasta dishes, introduced during Italian colonization, remain
  common, especially in the south.”
- **Copula avoidance:** “Gallery 825 serves as LAAA's exhibition space for
  contemporary art. The gallery features four separate spaces and boasts over
  3,000 square feet.” Prefer: “Gallery 825 is LAAA's exhibition space for
  contemporary art. The gallery has four rooms totaling 3,000 square feet.”
- **Negative parallelism:** “It's not just about the beat riding under the
  vocals; it's part of the aggression and atmosphere. It's not merely a song,
  it's a statement.” Prefer: “The heavy beat adds to the aggressive tone.” For
  a tailing negation, change “The options come from the selected item, no
  guessing.” to “The options come from the selected item without forcing the
  user to guess.”
- **Rule of three:** “The event features keynote sessions, panel discussions,
  and networking opportunities. Attendees can expect innovation, inspiration,
  and industry insights.” Prefer: “The event includes talks and panels. There's
  also time for informal networking between sessions.”
- **Synonym cycling:** “The protagonist faces many challenges. The main
  character must overcome obstacles. The central figure eventually triumphs.
  The hero returns home.” Prefer: “The protagonist faces many challenges but
  eventually triumphs and returns home.”
- **False range:** “Our journey through the universe has taken us from the
  singularity of the Big Bang to the grand cosmic web, from the birth and death
  of stars to the enigmatic dance of dark matter.” Prefer: “The book covers the
  Big Bang, star formation, and current theories about dark matter.”
- **Passive or subjectless fragments:** Change “No configuration file needed.
  The results are preserved automatically.” to “You do not need a
  configuration file. The system preserves the results automatically.”

### Style and formatting

- **Dashes:** Change “The term is primarily promoted by Dutch institutions—not
  by the people themselves. You don't say ‘Netherlands, Europe’ as an
  address—yet this mislabeling continues—even in official documents.” to “The
  term is primarily promoted by Dutch institutions, not by the people
  themselves. You don't say ‘Netherlands, Europe’ as an address, yet this
  mislabeling continues in official documents.” Also rewrite spaced em dashes
  and double hyphens used as dashes. Scan the final text for `—` and `–`, except
  when a supplied voice sample intentionally uses them.
- **Boldface:** Change “It blends **OKRs (Objectives and Key Results)**,
  **KPIs (Key Performance Indicators)**, and visual strategy tools such as the
  **Business Model Canvas (BMC)** and **Balanced Scorecard (BSC)**.” to “It
  blends OKRs, KPIs, and visual strategy tools like the Business Model Canvas
  and Balanced Scorecard.”
- **Inline-header lists:** Collapse “**User Experience:** ...;
  **Performance:** ...; **Security:** ...” into prose when the list adds no
  useful structure.
- **Title case:** Change “## Strategic Negotiations And Global Partnerships”
  to “## Strategic negotiations and global partnerships.”
- **Decorative emoji:** Replace “🚀 **Launch Phase:** The product launches in
  Q3 / 💡 **Key Insight:** Users prefer simplicity / ✅ **Next Steps:** Schedule
  follow-up meeting” with direct prose when the symbols add no meaning.
- **Curly quotes:** Do not treat curly quotes alone as an AI signal. Convert
  them only when the requested format or a cluster of other tells warrants it.

### Communication, hedging, and structure

- **Chatbot artifacts:** Replace “Here is an overview of the French
  Revolution. I hope this helps! Let me know if you'd like me to expand on any
  section.” with direct content such as “The French Revolution began in 1789
  when financial crisis and food shortages led to widespread unrest.”
- **Cutoff disclaimer:** Replace “While specific details about the company's
  founding are not extensively documented in readily available sources, it
  appears to have been established sometime in the 1990s.” with “The company's
  founding date is not documented in the available sources.” State a date only
  if a source provides one.
- **Speculative gap-fill:** Replace “Information about her early life is not
  publicly available, suggesting she maintains a low profile and keeps personal
  details private. She likely grew up in a middle-class household, which
  shaped her later interest in education reform.” with “Her early life is not
  documented in the available sources,” or omit the section.
- **Sycophancy:** Replace “Great question! You're absolutely right that this
  is a complex topic. That's an excellent point about the economic factors.”
  with “The economic factors you mentioned are relevant here.”
- **Filler:** Prefer “To achieve this,” “Because it was raining,” “Now,” “If
  you need help,” “The system can process,” and “The data shows” over the
  longer equivalent phrases.
- **Excessive hedging:** Change “It could potentially possibly be argued that
  the policy might have some effect on outcomes.” to “The policy may affect
  outcomes.”
- **Generic conclusion:** Cut “The future looks bright for the company.
  Exciting times lie ahead as they continue their journey toward excellence.
  This represents a major step in the right direction.” unless the source
  states concrete plans.
- **Hyphenated pairs:** Keep hyphens in attributive compounds such as “a
  high-quality, data-driven report,” but generally write “the report is high
  quality” and “the methodology is data driven” in predicate position.
- **Authority tropes:** Change “The real question is whether teams can adapt.
  At its core, what really matters is organizational readiness.” to “The
  question is whether teams can adapt. That mostly depends on whether the
  organization is ready to change its habits.”
- **Signposting:** Replace “Let's dive into how caching works in Next.js.
  Here's what you need to know.” with direct content such as “Next.js caches
  data at multiple layers, including request memoization, the data cache, and
  the router cache.”
- **Fragmented headers:** Remove a one-line warm-up such as “Speed matters.”
  between `## Performance` and the actual statement.
- **Diff anchoring:** Change “This function was added to replace the previous
  approach of iterating through all items, which caused O(n²) performance.” to
  “This function uses a hash map for O(1) lookups, avoiding the O(n²) cost of
  naive iteration,” unless the document is a changelog, release note, or
  migration guide.
- **Manufactured punchlines:** Change “Then AlphaEvolve arrived. It had no
  preference for symmetry. No aesthetic prior. No nostalgia for human taste.
  The old rules were gone.” to “AlphaEvolve changed the search because it did
  not favor symmetry or human-looking designs. That made some of the older
  assumptions less useful.”
- **Aphorisms:** Change “Symmetry is the language of trust. Efficiency becomes
  a trap when teams forget the human layer.” to “Symmetric layouts often feel
  more predictable to users. Teams can over-optimize workflows and miss how
  people actually use them.”
- **Fake-candid openers:** Change “Is it worth the price? Honestly? It depends
  on how often you'll use it.” to “Whether it's worth the price depends on how
  often you'll use it.”
