# Content intent and pillars

Use this reference when the request is about a recurring LinkedIn content
system, not only one isolated post. These labels organize editorial choices;
they are not predictions of reach, engagement, leads, or revenue.

## Choose one primary intent

Select the intent that best matches the reader's next useful step:

- **Discovery:** make a specific problem, distinction, or point of view easy
  for the right audience to recognize. Do not use bait, manufactured
  controversy, or broad claims merely to increase distribution.
- **Authority:** teach a mechanism, decision, tradeoff, or limitation that
  demonstrates real understanding. Ground technical or current claims in the
  evidence ledger.
- **Conversion:** help an already relevant reader evaluate an approved offer,
  resource, case study, or conversation. State the offer and next step plainly;
  do not imply outcomes that the evidence cannot support.
- **Relationship:** make a real observation, lesson, or professional context
  more familiar to the intended audience. Personal detail is optional and must
  come from supplied experience; do not manufacture vulnerability or a story.

Do not force every post into conversion. A discovery or authority post may
end with a takeaway or honest question. A conversion post needs a real,
approved destination. A relationship post still needs relevance to the
audience rather than becoming unrelated autobiography.

## Build a small pillar map

When planning a series, choose roughly three to five pillars from the user's
actual expertise, supplied work, recurring audience problems, or verified
research. For each pillar record:

1. the primary audience and problem;
2. the user's legitimate relationship to the subject;
3. the evidence or examples that can support it;
4. the safe post formats and angles it can produce; and
5. the useful reader action or approved handoff.

Pillars are a coverage map, not a quota. Do not prescribe a universal content
mix, posting frequency, time of day, hashtag count, or ratio between intents.
Change a pillar when the evidence, offer, audience, or user's actual work
changes. Do not create a pillar solely because it is popular or appears in a
creator template.

## Recognition without overclaiming

Use the audience's real terminology, objects, constraints, and decision context
when they are present in supplied material, observed conversations, or reliable
research. Specificity should help the right reader recognize relevance; it must
not pretend to know their budget, role, pain, or buying intent.

Separate three things in the draft and in reporting:

- distribution signals such as impressions or reactions;
- quality signals such as relevant comments, profile visits, or replies; and
- business outcomes such as a qualified conversation, meeting, proposal, or
  client.

Do not turn one into another, and do not treat a creator's case study,
benchmark, or framework as a universal law without independent evidence.

## Handoff boundaries

Profile optimization, offer design, funnel architecture, and performance
reporting are separate concerns. A post may point to an approved profile,
resource, or conversation, but `linkedin-post` must not silently rewrite a
profile, invent a lead magnet, or claim that a post converted. Use
`linkedin-workspace` for operational attribution and state.
